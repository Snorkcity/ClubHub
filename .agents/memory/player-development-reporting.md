---
name: Player Development reporting
description: Confirmed product direction for periodic coach assessments, access, scoring and generated reports.
---

Use **Player Development** as the feature name. Coaches run roughly three
reporting cycles annually: after pre-season, mid-season and end-of-season.

The confirmed seven categories are:

1. Technical ability
2. Tactical understanding
3. Physical development
4. Coachability and mindset
5. Effort and consistency
6. Teamwork and communication
7. Attendance and reliability, including punctuality

Show each category with its short practical description. Use one common 1–5
rubric based on the standard expected for that team's age and competition
level: 1 significant development required, 2 developing toward the standard,
3 meets the expected standard, 4 above it, and 5 exceptional at that level.
Three is a positive, expected result.

Do not force a ranking curve, and do not score players relative to their own
previous performance. Multiple players may receive the same rating when they
meet the same standard. Hide previous-cycle scores during initial assessment
to reduce anchoring; use them afterward for development trends.

Cycle access is explicit. The initiating coach chooses the coaches who may view
and edit; managers and unrelated staff do not inherit access. A nominated
Technical Director or account may receive the completed team report without
editing rights. Family reports are restricted to the player's authorised
guardians.

Assessors save players individually, then submit and lock the cycle once every
player is complete. Reports use coach-selected ratings to assemble category
narratives, but coaches preview and may edit wording before sending. Clearly
disclose that coaches performed the assessment and Nahreo helped generate the
written summary.

Keep the family report compact, ideally one page. It is a point-in-time
development update highlighting what is progressing well and what may be
slipping, not a selection certificate, comparative ranking, or formal document
to use against the club. Do not expose individual coach scores, internal notes,
team distributions, other-player comparisons, selection predictions, or
language implying future placement. Keep those details in the restricted
Technical Director report.

**Why:** Consistent team-level standards make ratings credible and help set
realistic family expectations, while narrow access protects sensitive youth
development information.

**How to apply:** Use these rules when designing the tab, permissions, database
model, score guidance, cycle workflow, PDF/secure-link delivery, and future
trend views.
